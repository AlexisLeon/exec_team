# 48-hour standup with a scored commitment ledger

## Decision

Every two days, each person commits to one shipped outcome due at the next standup.
Commitments are self-chosen, verifiable by someone else in ten seconds, and scored
yes/no in `company/momentum.md`. The founder is in the ledger on the same terms.
Hit rate becomes a tracked metric on the weekly scorecard. Run by `/standup`,
owned by the cofounder agent.

## Context

Team output is the company's binding constraint — the founder does 10–14 focused hours
a day against roughly 2–3 from the team. Alex has seen this work: at a VC fund he
worked at, a 48-hour standup produced a team that consistently did what it said and
more. The pattern is worth copying deliberately rather than approximately.

## Options considered

1. **Weekly commitments only** — the existing `/weekly`. A week gives four days of
   drift and one of sprint, and the drift is invisible until Friday.
2. **Daily standup** — higher overhead than the work warrants at this size, and daily
   cycles push commitments down to task-sized items that don't ship anything.
3. **48-hour cycle with a scored ledger (chosen)** — short enough that drift has
   nowhere to hide, long enough that a commitment is a real outcome.

## Why

Three mechanisms, not one, and the ritual fails if any is dropped:
self-set commitments create ownership where assigned work creates compliance;
binary scoring makes misses real; a written ledger gives the pattern memory that makes
"that's the third time" a sentence someone can actually say. A standup where misses
evaporate is worse than no standup — it teaches that commitments carry no weight.

## What we expect to happen

By **2026-11-24** (four weeks, ~14 cycles): commitment hit rate above 70% team-wide,
measured from `company/momentum.md`. Self-reported focused hours up from 2–3 toward 5+.
At least 12 of 14 standups actually run.

## What would make us reverse this

Standups stop happening within three weeks, or hit rate sits near 100% while output
doesn't visibly change — which would mean people are sandbagging commitments to keep
the streak clean, and the ledger is measuring the wrong thing. If someone's hit rate
stays low against commitments they wrote themselves, that is no longer a systems
problem and becomes a people decision.
