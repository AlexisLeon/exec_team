# Founding strategy — Digital Dental Lab

## Decision

1. **Goal: 12 active clinics by 2027-04-27**, from 1 today. Active = sends the minimum
   monthly consumption. **The milestone that counts is clinic two, arm's length** —
   two clinics at minimum clears breakeven.
2. **ICP: private practices with a high-ticket case mix**, owner-dentist signing and
   feeling the pain. Case mix, not chair count. DSOs disqualified for now.
3. **Lead with printed restorations.** Aligners for volume, guards for margin and easy
   first orders, crowns as base load. Sell **case margin and turnaround**, never the
   material.
4. **Do not buy the mill.** Keep the zirconia partner. Revisit when real clinic volume
   justifies it.
5. **Reprice the minimum consumption above breakeven** (18,000 against ~29,900 fixed).
6. **Quality tripwire: loss rate above 15% of potential monthly revenue stops new
   clinic signings.** Currently ~37%.
7. **Invest in characterization capability (Laura) over equipment.**

## Context

One active clinic, which Alex part-owns, whose ordering dentist is exiting — so **zero
arm's-length customers.** One clinic already churned when it could not meet the minimum
and the scanner was recovered, with Alex covering the shortfall personally. Best month
on record lost ~37% of potential revenue to process failures. Alex had been describing
his constraint as an inability to produce zirconia and e.max.

## Options considered

1. **Buy a mill and become full-service now** — spends scarce capital on demand that
   hasn't appeared, in a process where Alex is unskilled and iteration takes two days,
   while the scanner model already drains cash.
2. **Keep growing on the existing minimum and model** — every clinic signed at 18,000
   is served at a loss, and the hardware financing has already failed once at n=2.
3. **Fix the economics, scope to what we control, and chase clinic two (chosen).**

## Why

The binding constraint was misidentified. Both dentists who ever bought chose printed
restorations for **case margin**, not materials; zero customers have needed ceramics.
The real constraints are (a) selling a product dentists can't evaluate — solved by
selling economics, not chemistry — and (b) destroying a third of revenue in production.
Growth into an unfixed quality problem is how a lab loses a market where everyone
knows everyone.

## What we expect to happen

By **2026-11-24** (4 weeks):
- At least **1 arm's-length clinic** signed and ordering. This is the single test.
- Minimum consumption repriced above breakeven, in writing.
- Loss rate measured monthly and **below 15%**.
- Design approval checkpoint in place; post-mortem on the 9,000 MXN prosthesis written.
- Ten named ICP-qualified clinics in Attio.
- Longevity log started, 1-year posterior bridge backfilled.

## What would make us reverse this

- Clinics sign but can't reach a repriced minimum → the scanner-for-volume model is
  wrong for this market, and we move to rental or straight lab sales.
- Printed restorations fail in the longevity log → the thesis is gone; ceramics become
  mandatory and the mill decision reopens immediately.
- Loss rate stays above 15% with Laura in place → the quality capability isn't there
  yet and growth stops until it is.
