# X-ray lab — channel partner, not a clinic

> **Classification: CHANNEL, not a customer.** They own the relationship with their
> doctors. They do not count toward the goal of 12 active clinics. Track separately.

## Situation

An imaging lab that books appointments for patients, doctors and orthodontists, sends
the patient in for panoramic and CBCT scans, and keeps the studies. They sit in front
of a set of orthodontists we would otherwise have to reach one at a time.

The opportunity: produce **printed dental models** from their scans, to replace the
alginate + yeso (plaster) models that orthodontists use for ortho studies today.

## Pain being solved

Alginate and plaster models break and are imprecise. The lab is already capturing the
digital scan; the physical model is the weak link in a pipeline that is otherwise
already digital.

## Why this is a wedge

- They already hold the doctor relationship and the scan data.
- Printed models are produced on **3D printing capacity we fully control** — the one
  production line that is reliable today (see
  `2026-10-27-omnidental-rosario-enriquez.md`).
- Once we are producing their models, we have a path to the individual orthodontists
  behind them.

## Open questions — answer before this is real

- **Name and contact of the lab?**
- **How many orthodontists send them work?** This number determines whether this
  channel is worth more than several direct clinics.
- **Volume: studies per month?**
- **Price per model, and our margin?**
- **What does the lab get?** A referral reward / revenue share is cheaper and better
  aligned than running ads to doctors whose relationship someone else owns.
- **Who at the lab has to say yes?**

## Next step

TK — needs an owner and a date.

## What this changes

Confirms that channel partners may be a faster route to clinics than one-at-a-time
outbound, and that printed models are a second product line running on the
capacity we already control.
