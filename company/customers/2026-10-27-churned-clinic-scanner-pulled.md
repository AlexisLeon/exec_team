# Churned clinic — scanner pulled for under-consumption

**The most important file in this folder.** One churn at n=2 customers is a 50% failure
rate on the core business model, and it failed on the exact mechanism the whole GTM
thesis depends on.

## What happened

- Clinic received an intraoral scanner in exchange for a committed minimum monthly
  lab consumption.
- They **could not meet the minimum**, month after month.
- The scanner was therefore not being paid off. The founder covered the shortfall
  **out of pocket**.
- The scanner was recovered and the relationship ended.

## What he was buying

Like Rosario, he was betting **100% on 3D printed permanent restorations** — explicitly
to increase his margin per case. This was before we had a zirconia partner, so printed
resin was effectively the only product.

## Why this matters more than it looks

1. **The scanner-for-volume model has already failed once, at our smallest scale.**
   The goal of 12 active clinics means 12 scanners. If the minimum is set above what a
   typical target clinic can consume, we are financing hardware for clinics that
   cannot pay it off — and the founder absorbs the gap.
2. **It gives us a disqualifier.** There is a clinic size below which this model does
   not work. We do not currently know where that line is. Finding it is ICP work.
3. **The product was not the problem.** He wanted printed restorations and bet on them.
   He simply did not have the case volume.

## Open questions

- What was the minimum, in units and in pesos?
- What did he actually consume per month?
- How many chairs / patients per week did he have? **This is the ICP question.**
- Was the minimum ever renegotiated to something he could meet?
- Would he come back on a different structure — rental, lower minimum, no scanner?

## What this changes

Directly informs the ICP disqualifier in `strategy.md` §3 and the minimum-consumption
number. Raise before deploying scanner number three.
