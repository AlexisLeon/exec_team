Researched 2026-10-27 at Alex's request, against BEGO VarseoSmile TriniQ,
Rodin Titan and Rodin Sculpture 2.0.

> [Revision — 2026-10-27](#revision--2026-10-27-primary-source-check) first; it is

## The headline finding

**Nobody in this category has multi-year clinical survival data.** Every one of these
materials reached market through FDA 510(k) substantial equivalence, which for Class II
dental devices generally requires **bench testing, not clinical trials**. Rodin Titan's
clearance rests on ISO 20795-1 and ISO 4049 bench data. The published evidence is
manufacturer flexural modulus and wear figures, not survival curves.

**Two consequences, and they point in opposite directions.**

*The risk is real and unquantified.* We are selling permanence on bench data in a
category too young to have proven it. If these fail at 24–36 months, it will not be
one annoyed dentist — it will be our reputation in a market where everyone knows
everyone.

*The opportunity is also real.* If nobody has clinical data, then **our longevity log
is not an internal chore — it is the only clinical evidence base in our market.** One
3-unit posterior bridge at 1 year, with no failure, is already a stronger data point
than anything a competitor can show a dentist. In 24 months, a logged, photographed
series of placements is an asset no incumbent will have built, and it is close to free
to produce. See open item #9.

## Material by material

### Rodin Titan (Pac-Dent) — FDA K240688, cleared 2024-06-20

- Indicated as a **permanent restorative**: anterior restorations including occlusal
  surfaces, permanent inlays, veneers and full crowns; also hybrid and
  implant-supported denture prosthetics.
- Clearance by substantial equivalence; **bench testing only** (ISO 20795-1, ISO 4049).
- Notable for us: **explicitly cleared for permanent veneers and anterior.**

### Rodin Sculpture 2.0 (Pac-Dent) — crown-focused

- **>60% ceramic filler** (original Sculpture >50%), biaxial strength quoted **>200 MPa**.
- Carbon lists flexural modulus 6500 MPa, wear 1.4 mg / million cycles — manufacturer data.
- Marketed for permanent and temporary restorations; >50% filler is the basis for the
  claim that permanent crowns qualify for ADA insurance codes.
- **Highest filler load of the three. On bench data, the most defensible choice for
  posterior permanent crowns** — which is exactly where our evidence is strongest.

### BEGO VarseoSmile TriniQ — FDA K233596

- **Flag:** the GUDID device records list the FDA product code as
  **"Crown And Bridge, Temporary, Resin."** Verify directly with BEGO before making a
  permanence claim on this material. This may be a coding artifact — but if it isn't,
  there is regulatory and liability exposure in claiming permanence.
- A clinical trial is registered (**NCT07228572**, Ege University): 60 posterior
  single-tooth crowns in a resin nano-ceramic, run June 2025 – June 2026, assessed on
  modified USPHS and revised FDI criteria at baseline, 6 and 12 months. Panavia V5
  cement. **Results not yet published** — worth watching, it is the closest thing to
  real evidence in the category.

## Recommendation

1. **Default to Rodin Sculpture 2.0 for posterior permanent crowns.** Highest filler,
   best bench numbers, crown-focused, permanent indication.
2. **Resolve the TriniQ "temporary" product code with BEGO in writing** before using it
   for anything sold as permanent. Written answer, kept on file.
3. **Reconsider anterior.** Titan is explicitly cleared for permanent anterior and
   veneers. Our six veneer failures are therefore more likely **process** — design
   thickness, bonding protocol, cure, finishing — than material. Before scoping
   anterior out permanently, run a controlled test with Laura (see
   `persons/laura_garcia.md`): same material, same protocol, several units, and find
   out which step fails. Anterior is where the margin and the reputation both are.
4. **Start the longevity log this week.** Date, tooth, material, cement, clinic, photo.
   Backfill the 3-unit posterior bridge at 1 year.
5. **Match cement protocol to the published work** where possible — the Ege trial uses
   Panavia V5. Cement choice is a common failure variable and is cheap to standardize.

# Revision — 2026-10-27 (primary-source check)

Second pass, triggered by a re-ask on "is there a better resin than TriniQ."
This pass read the **FDA 510(k) clearance letters and Indications for Use forms
directly** instead of GUDID records and trade press. Three corrections.

## Correction 1 — TriniQ is cleared for permanent bridges. The flag was wrong.

K233596, Indications for Use, verbatim:

> "VarseoSmile TriniQ is indicated as an indirect restorative for both anterior and
> posterior restorations, including occlusal surfaces. The VarseoSmile TriniQ material
> is used for fabricating permanent restorations such as inlays, onlays, veneers,
> **full crown and bridge restorations.** VarseoSmile TriniQ can also be used for the
> fabrication of artificial teeth and temporary crowns & bridges."

The clearance letter lists **Product Code: EBF, EBG, PZY** — EBF (Material, Tooth
Shade, Resin) primary, EBG (Crown And Bridge, Temporary, Resin) and PZY (denture
tooth) secondary. Regulation 21 CFR 872.3690, Class II, cleared 2023-11-09.
Predicate: saremco print CROWNTEC (K213343).

**So the "temporary" code was a coding artifact**, exactly as the first pass
suspected but could not resolve: EBG is present because TriniQ is *also* indicated
for temporary crowns and bridges, alongside the permanent indication. The GUDID
record lists all three codes without hierarchy, which is what caused the false alarm.

Consequence: **open item #14 is downgraded.** No regulatory blocker on claiming
permanence for TriniQ in the US. The COFEPRIS question for Mexico is separate and
still open — that is about registration in our market, not about FDA clearance.

## Correction 2 — Rodin Titan is a denture resin, not a crown-and-bridge resin.

K240688 is regulated under **21 CFR 872.3760 — Denture Relining, Repairing, or
Rebasing Resin**, product code EBI. Its indications lead with hybrid denture
prosthetics, implant-supported denture prosthetics and monolithic removable denture
teeth. A permanent anterior restorative indication is in there, but the device's
regulatory identity is a denture material.

Consequence: **first-pass recommendation #3 was wrong to reach for Titan as the
anterior fix.** The underlying argument still holds — six veneer failures on a
material cleared for veneers points at process, not material — but the controlled
test with Laura (open item #16) should run on a crown-and-bridge resin, and TriniQ
is already cleared for permanent veneers and anterior.

## Correction 3 — clinical survival data now exists. The headline is out of date.

The first pass said nobody in the category has multi-year clinical survival data.
That was true until roughly this year. It no longer is:

| Study | n | Follow-up | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Del Piñal Pellón et al., *Clin Oral Investig* 2026 — posterior printed resin-matrix ceramic crowns | 30 | **2 years** | **93% survival, 87% success**, 2 debonds, no biological complications, no control group |
| Sonkaya & Bek Kürklü, *Int J Prosthodont* 2025 — overlays + endocrowns (Formlabs PCR), 4 premolars / 29 molars | 33 in 30 patients | **2 years** | ≥99.5% of modified USPHS scores in A/B; **marginal discoloration the only criterion that changed significantly** |
| Posterior 3D-printed resin composite **fixed dental prosthesis** (bridge) prospective trial | — | 1 year | directly relevant to our bridge work; full text not yet read |
| NCT07228572, Ege University — 60 posterior resin-nanoceramic crowns, Panavia V5 | 60 | 12 months | ran to June 2026, **results still unpublished** |

Three things follow.

1. **The dominant failure mode in the published data is debonding and marginal
   breakdown, not fracture.** Both 2-year studies point at the margin and the bond.
   That makes **cement protocol and finishing the controlled variables that matter**,
   which is a process lever we own, not a material property we buy.
2. **Our longevity log is still worth building, but it is no longer the only evidence
   in the market.** It is now a local, named, photographed series in *our* clinics —
   still an asset, more credible than bench data, no longer unique. Open item #9 keeps
   its priority; the claim attached to it has to get more modest.
3. **`strategy.md` §8 bet #2 and §5 both rest on "nobody has clinical data."**
   That premise has moved. Strategy is Alex's file and changes with a logged decision —
   flagging, not editing.

## The field, as of today

Cleared indications, from the IFU where we have it. **This is the column that decides
our purchase, because our case mix includes bridges.**

| Material | Filler | Strength (vendor) | Permanent indication | Bridges? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **BEGO VarseoSmile TriniQ** | "ceramic-filled", % not published | 120 MPa flex / 3600 MPa modulus | inlays, onlays, veneers, full crowns **and bridges** | **Yes — the only one. BEGO states up to 3 units** (7 units temporary) |
| saremco print CROWNTEC (K213343) | not published | >130–135 MPa flex (ISO 4049), E >4000 MPa | inlays, onlays, veneers, full crowns | No — temporary only |
| Rodin Sculpture 2.0 (Pac-Dent) | **>60%** | >200 MPa **biaxial** | marketed permanent, crown-focused | Not confirmed — no 510(k) located for 2.0 |
| SprintRay Ceramic Crown | >50% | ISO 4049 compliant, no MPa in IFU | single crowns, partial crowns (onlay/crownlay), veneers | No |
| **SprintRay Midas Restore** | **>75%** | 5× the wear resistance of Ceramic Crown at 200k cycles (vendor) | single crowns, inlays, onlays, veneers | No |
| Formlabs Permanent Crown V1 | — | — | — | **Discontinued — direct sales end 2026-03-01; Formlabs points users at TriniQ** |

Caveats that matter when reading that table:
- **>200 MPa biaxial is not comparable to 120 MPa three-point flexural.** Different
  test geometry, systematically higher numbers. Treat any cross-brand strength
  comparison between the two as meaningless.
- Vendor numbers run high. Independent work measured **CROWNTEC at 102 MPa** against a
  vendor claim of >135. Assume the same discount applies to everyone, including BEGO.
- One lab study on microhardness ranked **TriniQ lowest** of the materials tested
  (Cerasmart highest). Lowest hardness in a set is a wear signal, not a failure.
- **Midas Restore is locked to the SprintRay Midas Digital Press and NanoCure
  workflow.** The best filler load in the category is not purchasable as a resin; it
  is purchasable as a hardware commitment. Given that working capital is the most
  likely cause of death (`strategy.md` §8), that is the reason to pass, not the
  chemistry.

## Recommendation — stay on TriniQ

1. **Do not switch.** TriniQ is the **only resin in this field whose FDA Indications
   for Use name permanent bridge restorations.** Our case mix is inlays, crowns and
   bridges. Every candidate that beats it on filler load — Sculpture 2.0, Ceramic
   Crown, Midas Restore — is single-unit-or-less by indication. Switching to win a
   filler-percentage argument would mean giving up the one indication we actually
   need, and the one differentiated claim in `strategy.md` §5.
2. **Hold BEGO to the 3-unit limit in writing, with the pontic count.** The IFU says
   "bridge restorations" without a number; the 3-unit figure is BEGO's product
   literature, not the clearance. Get it from BEGO in writing and keep it on file —
   that letter is what we show a dentist who asks. **Alex, by 2026-11-05** (replaces
   open item #14's original purpose).
3. **The real upgrade available to us is not a resin — it is the cement and the
   margin.** Both 2-year studies failed at the bond and the margin, not in the bulk
   material. Standardize on one adhesive protocol, write it down, and put cement in
   the longevity log as a logged field. Cheaper than a material change and it attacks
   the published failure mode.
4. **If we ever add a second resin, add it for single posterior crowns only**, on
   measured wear, and keep TriniQ for anything with a pontic. Do not run two materials
   before the longevity log can tell them apart — a second material halves the n on
   both.
5. **Revisit when the Ege results publish** (NCT07228572, resin nanoceramic posterior
   crowns, Panavia V5, completed June 2026). That is the closest published work to
   what we sell.
