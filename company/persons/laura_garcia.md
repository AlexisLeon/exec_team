# Laura García — Characterization technician

> Created 2026-10-27. Comes from an **artistry background — she paints, and paints well.**

## Why she may be the most important person in the company

Alex's stated limitation, in his own words, is that he cannot produce exceptional
quality himself and that learning ceramics and characterization is a poor use of his
hours — expensive to learn, slow feedback loop, a ruined crown every two days.

Laura is that capability. Characterization and glazing is exactly the gap.

And the artistry background is the right background. Shade matching, layering and
characterization are closer to painting than to engineering. A technician who can
actually *see* colour is rarer and harder to hire than someone who can run a printer.

**The company's differentiation is quality and consistency. She is the person who
produces it.** That should shape how she's compensated, how fast she's trained up,
and how hard she'd be to replace.

## The open question that matters most

The six botched printed veneers and the anterior aesthetic failures are
**characterization problems**. So:

- Were those cases hers, or were they before she started / outside her scope?
- If they were hers: is it training, materials, or process? She comes from art,
  not dentistry — the dental-specific technique may simply not be there yet.
- If they weren't: **can she fix anterior aesthetics?** That would reopen a product
  line currently being scoped out, and anterior is where the margin and the
  reputation both live.

Answering this decides whether anterior aesthetics is permanently out of scope or
just paused.

A mill produces a material the customers haven't asked for; Laura produces the
