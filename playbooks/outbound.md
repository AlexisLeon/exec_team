# Outbound playbook

Written for a technical founder who has only ever done inbound. The whole point of
outbound is to stop waiting. You already know how to create value; outbound is
just deciding *who* gets to hear about it, instead of letting Google decide.

**Status: draft.** The GTM agent maintains this file. It gets more specific every
week as real reply data comes in.

## The mental model

Inbound: you build something good, people arrive already convinced, you capture value.
Outbound: you pick 50 companies that *should* be convinced, and you earn 15 minutes.

The two failure modes for technical founders:
1. **Treating it as a volume problem.** 2,000 emails from a cold domain is not a
   motion, it's a spam complaint. At your stage, 20 researched emails a day beats
   500 automated ones.
2. **Treating it as a product demo.** They don't want to see the product. They want
   to know if you understand their problem. Lead with the problem.

## The founder-led sequence (weeks 1–8)

You do this yourself, by hand, until you have 20 qualified conversations. Do not
hire an SDR and do not buy a sequencer before then. You are buying *information*,
not meetings — what makes them reply, what objection comes up, what words they use.

**Daily, 90 minutes, non-negotiable, before anything else:**
- 30 min: add 10 accounts to the list, researched (not scraped)
- 45 min: send 10 first-touch emails, each with a real first line
- 15 min: work the follow-ups due today

**Weekly target to start:** 50 accounts, 50 first touches, ~5 replies, ~2 calls.
If reply rate is under 4%, the problem is the list or the first line, not the volume.

## Account selection

A company goes on the list only if you can answer all three:
1. Do they have the trigger? (see `company/strategy.md` §3)
2. Can you name the human who feels the pain daily?
3. Is there something specific and true you can say about *them* in one sentence?

If no to any, it's not a prospect, it's a name. Names waste your 90 minutes.

## The first touch

```
subject: <lowercase, 3-5 words, looks typed not generated>

<One sentence that proves you looked. Specific to them. No flattery.>

<One sentence: the problem you believe they have, in their language, from customers/.>

<One sentence: the result, with a number, for someone like them.>

<The ask: 15 minutes, with a reason it's worth it to them.>

<name>
```

Under 90 words. No attachments. No links on the first touch except your signature.

## The sequence

| Day | Touch | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Email 1 | Problem-led, as above |
| 3 | Email 2 | One proof point. A number, a before/after, a named result. 2 sentences. |
| 7 | Email 3 | Different angle — a second problem they might care about more |
| 12 | Email 4 | Something useful with no ask attached (a teardown, a benchmark, a number) |
| 18 | Email 5 | Close the loop: "I'll stop here — worth revisiting in Q2?" |

Then stop. Re-enter in 90 days with new information, not a new nudge.

## Qualification

Before you spend a second call: can they sign, is there a trigger, is there a date.
Missing any one of the three → it's not in pipeline. Be ruthless. A fake pipeline
is worse than an empty one, because you stop prospecting.

## What to measure (feeds `company/scorecard.md`)

- Accounts added · First touches sent · Reply rate · Positive reply rate
- Qualified conversations booked · Conversations → opportunity
- **Objection tally** — the most valuable number you'll collect. Count them. The top
  objection becomes next week's positioning work for the CMO.
